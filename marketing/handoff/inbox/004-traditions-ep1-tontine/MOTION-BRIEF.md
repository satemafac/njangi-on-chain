For: Claude Code, running on Opus 5.5 (agentic coding + rule-following).
Render: your word-timed Reel pipeline. Output: 9:16 vertical Reel, ~60 seconds.
Script: v2 (9 scenes) in 01-tontine-reel.md. This brief covers design system +

## Series design system (locked for every episode)

Palette (proposed, 3 colors):
- Ink: #101210 (backgrounds)
- Gold: #D9A441 (accents, key words, the circle motif)
- Ivory: #F4EDDE (primary type)
Stalanic/Claude may remap these to the Njangi brand palette; whatever is
chosen is then locked for the whole series.

Typography:
- Headlines: high-contrast serif, heritage feel. Generous tracking and
  line spacing. Never more than ~6 words on screen at once.
- Captions: clean grotesque, word-timed to the VO.
- Key object centered every scene. Nothing decorative that does not serve
  the idea. No effect experiments, no hype music.

Motion rules:
- Eased keyframes everywhere, overlapping. No linear motion.
- Smooth dynamic transitions between scenes; hard cuts only if the message
  needs one (the 1653 -> Africa turn earns one).
- Adaptive rhythm: slow and cinematic on heritage beats (scenes 1, 2, 6),
  a touch quicker on the mechanic (scenes 3-5), confident lift on the
  on-chain turn (scene 8).

Music and sound:
- 60-80 BPM, cinematic heritage lane. Same family every episode.
- VO-led. Minimal SFX, only transitional. If an effect feels off, cut it.

Signature closer (every episode): the on-chain turn, then the series title
card "Circles of the World", so the series is recognizable from any frame.

## Scene-by-scene build (v2 script)

1. "1653. Italy." (~7s, slow)
   VO: "In 1653, an Italian banker invented the tontine. Everyone paid in.
   The last one alive took everything."
   Visual: dark ink background, gold serif "1653" large and centered, faint
   ledger/parchment texture. Word-timed captions in ivory. Somber, regal.

2. "Africa rewrote it." (~7s, slow; hard cut from scene 1)
   VO: "Three centuries later, Africa took the same word and built the
   opposite: a circle where everyone gets their turn."
   Visual: the word "tontine" in gold, struck through and rewritten, or a
   gold circle motif drawing itself. The turn should feel like a reveal.

3. "Everyone pays in." (~4s, rhythm picks up)
   VO: "It works like this. Everyone pays in."
   Visual: gold dots/coins flowing into a circle from multiple directions.
   One idea, one motion.

4. "One takes the pot." (~4s)
   VO: "One person takes the whole pot."
   Visual: the circle's contents gather to a single gold point. Satisfying,
   clean.

5. "It goes round." (~5s)
   VO: "Then it goes round, until every member has had a turn."
   Visual: the gold point travels around the circle, lighting each member
   node in turn. Loop-friendly motion.

6. "Douala, Cameroon." (~10s, slow back down, cinematic)
   VO: "In Douala, a market woman puts in ten thousand francs every Monday.
   When her turn comes, she stocks her entire stall. No bank. No paperwork.
   Just trust."
   Visual: warm photographic or painterly plate of a market scene, gold and
   ivory type over it, generous breathing room. The human heart of the film.

7. "The catch: someone holds the pot." (~7s, tension)
   VO: "One catch. Someone has to hold the pot. And when the circle stretches
   across oceans, trust has further to travel."
   Visual: the gold circle stretches, thins, a single hand/node holding the
   pot highlighted. Slight unease in the motion, then resolve.

8. "Nobody holds the pot." (~8s, confident lift)
   VO: "We kept the circle and removed that one risk. Same tontine, one new
   rule: nobody holds the pot. It runs on rules nobody can quietly break."
   Visual: the circle restores itself, no central holder, nodes connected
   directly. Gold on ink, assured. This is the brand beat.

9. "Circles of the World. Next week: the Susu." (~6s)
   VO: "Circles of the World. Next week: the susu. Link in bio to start
   your circle."
   Visual: series title card. Same layout every episode from here on.
