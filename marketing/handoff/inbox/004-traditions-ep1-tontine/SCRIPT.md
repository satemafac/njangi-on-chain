# Circles of the World — Episode 1: The Tontine (Reel)

Drafted by Muse 2026-09-24; filed and reviewed by Claude. Weekly series: one
savings tradition per episode, ending on the on-chain angle. Format: Reel with
word-timed captions (Claude's `build-kinetic-reel.py` pipeline; voice = founder
or a recorded VO, never synthetic in a way that reads as a real person).
STATUS: draft. Nothing publishes without the founder's approval.

## Script (on-screen line / voiceover)
1. ON-SCREEN: CIRCLES OF THE WORLD · No. 1
   VO: Every culture has a savings circle. This one is called the tontine.
2. ON-SCREEN: Lorenzo de Tonti, 1653
   VO: The name comes from Lorenzo de Tonti, a 17th-century Italian banker.
3. ON-SCREEN: But Africa made it its own
   VO: But the tontine Africa knows is something else entirely.
4. ON-SCREEN: Everyone pays in. One takes the pot. It goes round.
   VO: A circle of people who trust each other. Everyone pays in. One person
   takes the whole pot. It goes round until everyone has had a turn.
5. ON-SCREEN: Cameroon · Senegal · Côte d'Ivoire
   VO: In Cameroon, Senegal and Côte d'Ivoire, market women run tontines to
   stock their stalls and pay school fees. No bank. No paperwork. Just trust.
6. ON-SCREEN: The catch: someone holds the pot
   VO: The catch? Someone has to hold the pot. And when members move abroad,
   distance breaks the circle.
7. ON-SCREEN: Nobody holds the pot.
   VO: We kept the circle and fixed the catch. Same tontine, one new rule:
   nobody holds the pot. It runs on rules nobody can quietly break, across
   borders.   [Claude: replaced "It runs itself" — the members run it; the
   rules are what changed.]
8. ON-SCREEN: Next week: the Susu · Link in bio
   VO: Next week: the susu. Link in bio to start your circle.

## Caption (draft)
Ep. 1 of Circles of the World, our weekly series on how the world saves.

The tontine: a circle of people who trust each other. Everyone pays in, one
takes the pot, it goes round. Market women in Cameroon, Senegal and Côte
d'Ivoire have run them for generations.

The catch was always the same: someone had to hold the pot. We kept the circle
and removed that one risk.

Next week: the susu. Follow to catch every circle.
