Ep. 1 of Circles of the World, our weekly series on how the world saves together.

In 1653, the tontine was a gamble: everyone paid in, and the last one alive took everything. In Africa, the same word came to mean the opposite: a circle where everyone gets their turn.

In Cameroon we also call it njangi, which is where our name comes from. In Douala, a market woman puts in 10,000 francs every Monday, and when her turn comes she stocks her whole stall. No bank. No paperwork. Just trust.

The catch was always the same: someone had to hold the pot. We kept the circle and removed that one risk. Same tontine, one new rule: nobody holds the pot.
