## Script (voiceover + on-screen lines)

1. On-screen: Paluwagan. The Philippines.
   VO: "In the Philippines, the circle is called paluwagan. And the payout
   has a name too. Sahod. The word for salary."

2. On-screen: A salary you pay yourselves.
   VO: "Because that is what it is. Friends, family, and coworkers paying
   each other a salary, one at a time."

3. On-screen: Everyone pays in.
   VO: "It works like this. Everyone pays in."

4. On-screen: One takes the sahod.
   VO: "One person takes the whole sahod."

5. On-screen: It goes round.
   VO: "Then it goes round, until every member has had a turn."

6. On-screen: Payday in Manila.
   VO: "Every payday, the collector gathers the hulog. The order is drawn
   by lots, or set by need. Everyone keeps paying, even after their turn.
   No bank. No paperwork. Just trust."

7. On-screen: The catch: someone holds the pot.
   VO: "One catch. Someone has to hold the pot. And when the circle
   stretches across oceans, trust has further to travel."

8. On-screen: Nobody holds the pot.
   VO: "We kept the circle and removed that one risk. Same paluwagan, one
   new rule: nobody holds the pot. It runs on rules nobody can quietly
   break."

9. On-screen: Circles of the World. Next week: the Tanda.
   VO: "Circles of the World. Next week: the tanda. Link in bio to start
   your circle."

## Notes

- Brand voice: first-person plural, no em dashes.
- Fact grounding (researched 2026-09-27): paluwagan is the Filipino rotating
  savings circle; the contribution is the hulog and the payout is the sahod
  (literally "salary"); the payout order is set by drawing lots or by
  need; members keep contributing after receiving; the collector gathers
  and distributes; the Philippine Supreme Court has ruled a paluwagan is
  not a game of chance. No founding date is documented, so none is claimed.
