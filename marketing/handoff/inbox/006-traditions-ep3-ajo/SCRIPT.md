# Traditions Series — Episode 3: The Ajo — SCRIPT

Series: Circles of the World. Weekly: one savings tradition per episode,
ending with the on-chain angle. Format: 9:16 vertical Reel, ~60 seconds,
word-timed captions (Claude's render pipeline, Opus 5.5).
Status: draft, needs Stalanic's approval before anything is published.

## Script (voiceover + on-screen lines)

1. On-screen: Ajo. Nigeria.
   VO: "Ajo. The Yoruba word for thrift. Across Nigeria, millions of people
   run their savings on it."

2. On-screen: The bank never came.
   VO: "The bank never came for the market trader. The ajo did."

3. On-screen: Everyone pays in.
   VO: "It works like this. Everyone pays in."

4. On-screen: One takes the pot.
   VO: "One person takes the whole pot."

5. On-screen: It goes round.
   VO: "Then it goes round, until every member has had a turn."

6. On-screen: The alajo's notebook.
   VO: "Every morning, the alajo walks his round with a notebook, collecting
   a fixed sum from each trader. At the end of the cycle, each trader gets
   their lump sum. His fee: one day's contribution."

7. On-screen: The catch: someone holds the pot.
   VO: "One catch. Someone has to hold the pot. And when the circle
   stretches across oceans, trust has further to travel."

8. On-screen: Nobody holds the pot.
   VO: "We kept the circle and removed that one risk. Same ajo, one new
   rule: nobody holds the pot. It runs on rules nobody can quietly break."

9. On-screen: Circles of the World. Next week: the Paluwagan.
   VO: "Circles of the World. Next week: the paluwagan. Link in bio to
   start your circle."

## Notes

- Brand voice: first-person plural, no em dashes.
- Fact grounding (researched 2026-09-27): "ajo" is glossed as thrift or
  microsavings in Yoruba; "millions of Nigerians" rely on rotating
  contributions; the alajo is the documented mobile collector who keeps one
  day's contribution as commission; related names include esusu (Igbo),
  adashe and akawo (Hausa). No founding date is documented, so none is
  claimed.
