# Fact-check: Circles of the World, Ep. 3 "The Ajo"

Checked 2026-10-03 against packet `marketing/handoff/inbox/006-traditions-ep3-ajo/`
(SCRIPT.md, VO-SCRIPT.md, CAPTION.txt, MOTION-BRIEF.md, MUSE-PROMPT.md; unchanged
since 2026-09-27, committed in PR #90). The packet has no JOB, BUILD or RESULT
file yet. Read-only: nothing in the repo was edited.

**Verdict: 32 claims. 20 correct, 10 need correction, 2 unverifiable.**
The caption repeats two script problems, so there are 6 distinct fixes:

1. The script presents two different practices as one ajo. Scenes 3 to 5 show a
   rotation. Scene 6's alajo is a daily collector who returns each trader's own
   money. That is not a rotation (Seibel 2006; Global Encyclopaedia of
   Informality; our own glossary). Scene 8 then says "Same ajo". **Fix:** add
   one bridge line, "Ajo has a second form", and say "their own money back".
2. Scene 2, "The bank never came for the market trader": this is literally
   false. **Fix:** replace it with a verifiable line. A Yoruba dictionary
   described these circles in 1852, and Nigeria's first bank opened in 1892.
3. Scene 1 and the caption, "The Yoruba word for thrift": the word àjọ means a
   gathering or assembly. "Thrift" is the Nigerian-English name for the
   practice, and to most viewers it means frugality.
4. Scene 1, "Across Nigeria ... run their savings on it": "ajo" is the Yoruba
   name only, and "run their savings" overstates. **Fix:** say "under many
   names" and "save this way".
5. Pronunciation guide: ajo is ah-JAW, not ah-JOH. Ep. 2's live sign-off used
   ah-JAW. Alajo is ah-LAH-jaw.
6. Internal notes: esusu is Yoruba, not Igbo. The Igbo name is isusu. "Akawo"
   appears in blogs only.

## 3. Where sources disagree, and what not to use

- **What "ajo" means.** Seibel (2006) and the Global Encyclopaedia of
  Informality use "ajo" for daily doorstep collection, separate from the
  rotating esusu. Nigerian press, fintech copy and a 2026 paper (POP26) use
  "ajo" for rotating group savings. Our glossary records both senses. "Ajo has a
  second form" is accurate under either reading.
- **Do not use Seibel's "16th century" date for esusu.** He cites no primary
  source for it. The earliest verified written description is 1852 (C1852;
  /learn/esusu already says "since at least 1852").
- **Do not use the Alajo Somolu legend yet.** He was a famous Lagos collector
  who reportedly learned the trade in Cameroon. But sources disagree on when he
  started (1950 or 1954) and on the Yoruba saying about him; one version mocks
  him (PULSE vs BD24).
- **EFInA 2023 slide 22 has a mislabelled legend.** Its "2023" bars carry the
  2020 report's 2018 values. Read the green bars as 2023, or quote the 2020
  final report instead.
- **The Caribbean link is not claimed in Ep. 3.** If it is added later, keep it
  hedged ("widely traced to"). Seibel cites Bascom 1952:69; GEI says the
  practice "presumably" crossed with the slave trade. I did not read Bascom
  directly.

## 5. Open questions for Stalanic

1. **Approve fix #1:** keep the alajo as "Ajo has a second form" (recommended).
   The alternative is to cut the alajo for a rotating vignette, but that would
   rest on unsourced specifics. Renaming the episode is not an option, because
   Ep. 2 already promised "the ajo".
2. **Approve swapping "The bank never came" for the 1852 / 1892 line.**
3. **Pronunciation.** Our glossary entry for ajo says 'AH-jaw', but Ep. 2's live
   sign-off was voiced ah-JAW. Use ah-JAW for Ep. 3, and align the glossary in
   a separate change.
4. **Keep "His fee: one day's contribution"?** I recommend keeping it: it is
   documented and shows what trust costs. Do not pair it with a "we charge
   nothing" line. Njangi has a subscription, so any claim about taking no cut
   of the pot needs invariant #3 wording and a separate review.
5. **Scene 8 is a product claim outside this history check.** Given the open,
   privately tracked contract item on old package versions, have engineering
   confirm that "rules nobody can quietly break" still stands before publishing.
6. **Optional enrichment.** The 1852 description was written by Samuel Ajayi
   Crowther. He was born around 1810 in Osogun, enslaved around 1821, freed by
   a British warship, and became the first African Anglican bishop in 1864
   (DACB). His entry also says that late-turn members saved what they would
   have spent "by little and little", which echoes Ep. 2. Use it in the caption
   or the first comment?
7. **"his round" / "His fee".** Alajo are men and women. Keep "his", or
   neutralize?
