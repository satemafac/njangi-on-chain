Ep. 3 of Circles of the World, our weekly series on how the world saves together.

Ajo is the Yoruba word for thrift. Across Nigeria, millions of people save in rotating circles: everyone pays in on a fixed schedule, one member takes the whole pot, and it goes round until every member has had a turn.

Every morning, the alajo walks his round with a notebook, collecting a fixed sum from each trader. At the end of the cycle, each trader gets their lump sum. His fee: one day's contribution.

The catch was always the same: someone had to hold the pot. We kept the circle and removed that one risk.
