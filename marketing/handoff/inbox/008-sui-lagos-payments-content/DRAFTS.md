## 1. LinkedIn post · company page

1,744 characters. No hashtags (our LinkedIn rule: none, or one or two at
most). The link goes in the body; LinkedIn allows it, and the argument is
complete without a click.

> At Sui Live in Miami this May, Paga founder Tayo Oviosu said financial freedom comes down to three things. Move money quickly and affordably. Transact globally, whatever the currency. Save.
>
> Communities across Africa have been doing the third one together for generations.
>
> In Lagos it is esusu. In Cameroon, where our name comes from, njangi. In Ghana, susu. Everyone pays in on a schedule, one member takes the whole pot, and it goes round until everyone has had a turn. Yoruba communities were running esusu long before any colonial bank reached the region.
>
> Tayo's talk was about infrastructure. Of Paga's 17 years he said, "We did not set out to build an app. We set out to build an infrastructure." The circle is infrastructure too. It runs on trust instead of rails, and it has one weak point every member knows: somebody has to hold the pot.
>
> He called slow, costly money "a tax on friction" that Africans have paid for generations. Circles pay their own version. The organiser carries everyone's cash. One notebook is the only record. When the notebook and someone's memory disagree, a friendship pays for it.
>
> So we kept the circle and changed one thing. Nobody holds the pot. Not the organiser, and not us. Each round's contributions sit where no company can reach them, only the member whose turn it is can collect the payout, and every member sees the same ledger.
>
> We build on Sui too, and we are live on testnet today. Tayo closed on 2050: 2.5 billion Africans, and 40% of all the young people on Earth. That generation will run its circles from a phone. We want those circles ready for them, with the trust intact and far less friction.
>
> The talk is 15 minutes, and worth all of them: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IQ851ZlNoh4
