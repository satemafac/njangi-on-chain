## Njangi tie-in angles (use, refine, or replace)

1. Move, transact, save. Tayo defines financial freedom as three things.
   We are the "save" layer for communities: rotating circles are
   Africa's original savings infrastructure, now on-chain.
2. The trust parallel. Paga spent 17 years building trusted rails. Susu,
   ajo, njangi circles are centuries-old trusted rails. The oldest African
   financial infrastructure, on the newest rails.
3. The tax on friction. Tayo: "a tax on friction, Africans have been
   paying for generations." Circles pay it too: collector risk, defaults,
   paper records. On-chain removes the single point of trust failure.
4. Currency erosion. The Lagos teacher whose lifetime savings devalued
   threefold in a year. A circle hand must hold its value; stable value
   protects the payout.
5. The demographic wave. 1.4B to 2.5B by 2050, 60% under 25, a generation
   with no patience for cash queues. They will run their circles on phones.
6. Same rails. Paga x Sui moves money; we move trust. Complementary
   layers, one infrastructure story.
7. The hospital anecdote. Communities invented circles for exactly the
   emergencies formal finance abandons. The payout hand has saved lives.
