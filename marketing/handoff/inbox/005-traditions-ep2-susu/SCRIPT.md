# Traditions Series — Episode 2: The Susu — SCRIPT

Series: Circles of the World. Weekly: one savings tradition per episode,
ending with the on-chain angle. Format: 9:16 vertical Reel, ~60 seconds,
word-timed captions (Claude's render pipeline, Opus 5.5).
Status: draft, needs Stalanic's approval before anything is published.

## Script (voiceover + on-screen lines)

1. On-screen: Susu. "Little by little."
   VO: "One word from Ghana: susu. It means little by little. A plan built
   from small pieces."

2. On-screen: It crossed the ocean.
   VO: "It crossed the Atlantic centuries ago, and it never died. Trinidad.
   Brooklyn. Anywhere the diaspora gathers."

3. On-screen: Everyone pays in.
   VO: "It works like this. Everyone pays in."

4. On-screen: One takes the hand.
   VO: "One person takes the whole hand."

5. On-screen: It goes round.
   VO: "Then it goes round, until every member has had a turn."

6. On-screen: The banker lady.
   VO: "In Trinidad, a banker lady collects from her neighbors every week.
   When your turn comes, the whole hand is yours at once. No bank. No
   paperwork. Just trust."

7. On-screen: The catch: someone holds the hand.
   VO: "One catch. Someone has to hold the hand. And when the circle
   stretches across oceans, trust has further to travel."

8. On-screen: Nobody holds the hand.
   VO: "We kept the circle and removed that one risk. Same susu, one new
   rule: nobody holds the hand. It runs on rules nobody can quietly break."

9. On-screen: Circles of the World. Next week: the Ajo.
   VO: "Circles of the World. Next week: the ajo. Link in bio to start your
   circle."

## Notes

- Brand voice: first-person plural, no em dashes.
- Fact grounding (researched 2026-09-27): "susu" means "little by little"
  and "to plan" in Twi; West African origin with the Caribbean form carried
  across the Atlantic; the "banker lady" is the documented organizer role
  in Trinidad; the payout is called a "hand." No founding date is
  documented, so none is claimed.
